# SITAR Adventure Design

**Draft for discussion**

SITAR Adventure connects all eleven existing games through *The Festival of Five Lights*. Each chapter gives the player a reason to practice a cognitive or motor skill. In several chapters, the player completes part of the mission on the TV and then uses physical pegs on a tabletop board to finish it. The existing game library provides direct access to individual activities for practice sessions.

## Story and chapters

A storm damages five lanterns just before the village festival. The player helps each neighborhood prepare for opening night. Each chapter restores one lantern and changes something visible in the village.

| Chapter | Mission and existing games | Skills practiced |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The Lantern Workshop | Find missing pieces in **Focus Finder**, then build a lantern pattern with physical pegs in **Shape Match**. The first lantern lights up. | Visual scanning, shape discrimination, grasp and placement |
| 2. The Music Garden | Match pairs in **Memory Meadow** and replay a tune in **Melody Steps**. Recreate a short pattern on the pegboard to restore the garden display. | Visual and auditory memory, sequencing, controlled placement |
| 3. The River Crossing | Identify a river sound in **Listen & Discover** and trace a route in **Connect & Draw**. Complete a bridge pattern on the pegboard to reopen the crossing. | Sound recognition, visual tracking, hand–eye coordination |
| 4. The Harbor and Woods | Steer the boat in **Ocean Rescue** and collect glowing seeds in **Seed Quest**. Boats return to the harbor and the path lights up. | Reaching, steering, aiming, sustained attention |
| 5. The Festival Market | Count supplies in **Quick Sums**, buy ingredients in **Market Basket**, and serve the opening meal in **Dosa Master**. The village celebrates under all five lanterns. | Numeracy, decision-making, monitoring multiple cues |

## Levels and difficulty

Each chapter starts with a guided task, moves to independent practice, then offers a challenge focused on the session goal. The facilitator can start directly at a chapter and level. Use large targets, short instructions, and optional hints. Support pauses and retries, and let players complete the story with guidance.

| Level | Lantern Workshop example |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Guided placement | Highlight one board position at a time. The player chooses and places the matching peg at their own pace. |
| 2. Copy a pattern | Show a short pattern throughout the task. The player places the pegs independently and can request a hint. |
| 3. Focused challenge | For memory practice, briefly hide the pattern while keeping the same layout. For motor practice, keep the pattern visible and spread placements across the board within comfortable reach. |

Adjust cognitive and motor demands independently. Cognitive settings can change the number of choices, sequence length, or cue visibility. Motor settings can change the placement spacing, reach, or number of transfers. For screen movement games, start with one large target and progress to several stops. For the tabletop prototype, keep a tabletop countdown off and use completion feedback to show progress.

## Screen and tabletop handoff

After the screen activity, show a single instruction such as **Build the lantern on your pegboard** and display the target pattern. The facilitator selects the matching board through the existing Shape Match setup. Proposed Adventure patterns and memory cues extend Shape Match. Confirm which placements the current hardware can verify before relying on automatic completion. Where a placement cannot be verified, let the facilitator confirm the result. Finish with the lantern lighting up so the player can see how the tabletop task advanced the mission.

## First playable chapter

Prototype the Lantern Workshop with Focus Finder, the existing Shape Match setup, and one physical board configuration. Reuse the shared game controls for pause and replay. Add the chapter introduction, screen-to-tabletop transition, and lantern reveal.

- Walk through the complete mission with the TV input and physical board together. Check that the player can tell when to switch to the tabletop and which pattern to build.
- Try guided placement, copying, and both focused challenge variants. Check that increasing the memory demand leaves the physical layout unchanged and increasing the reaching demand leaves the pattern visible.
- Check that hints, retries, and facilitator confirmation can lead to the same story reward. Record gameplay observations such as completed placements and hints used to inform design revisions.

## Prototype decisions

Choose the first physical board and peg pattern, confirm placement detection on that setup, and decide how the facilitator selects the memory or motor challenge. Use the first playtest to refine pattern length, cue timing, and comfortable reach before expanding to the other chapters.
