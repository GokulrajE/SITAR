# Journey Home: story bible

This expands the story in [JOURNEY_HOME_DESIGN.md](JOURNEY_HOME_DESIGN.md). The chapters,
movements and therapy goals stay the same. This file adds names, reasons, a twist, a
guardian for every seed, set pieces, rest scenes and the ending. The art prompts for
every picture named here are in [JOURNEY_HOME_ART_PROMPTS.md](JOURNEY_HOME_ART_PROMPTS.md).

## Changes from the design document

- **The companion is now Kili, a robot sparrow.** The design document called it Pip, but
  Pip is already the blue mascot of Shape Match. Two different "Pip"s would confuse
  patients who play both games. (*Kili* means parrot in Tamil and is easy to say.)
- **Captain Rust becomes a person who changes.** He starts as the pirate who stole the
  village's water and ends as the man who lights the festival fireworks.
- **The storm has a cause that is healed, not destroyed.** The cause is Megha, an old
  rain machine that is broken and sad. This matches the whole game: broken things are
  repaired by practice.
- **The hero's own arm was hurt in the storm.** The hero grows stronger chapter by
  chapter, as the player does. The wording is always "practice", never "cure".

## The world in one paragraph

Malarpuram ("flower town") is a farming village at the foot of green hills. Long ago the
village inventor, Grandpa Raman, built two machines. **Megha** brought gentle rain to the
fields on time every year. **Kili** was a little robot sparrow who sang to Megha to keep
her company. When Raman died, nobody looked after Megha. Rust, his clever but proud old
apprentice, tried to take her over and sell her rain. He broke her. Megha flew up into the
sky in a wild storm cloud, and every year since she has grown angrier and lonelier. This
year her storm struck the village itself.

## Characters

| Character | Who they are | What they want | How they change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Arun or Meera** (the player chooses) | A young farmer, about 25. Their right arm was hurt holding the granary door shut on the night of the storm. | To bring the village back to life. | Starts with a sling. After chapter 1 it becomes a wrist band, and in the finale they lift the festival lamp with both hands. |
| **Kili** | Raman's robot sparrow, found switched off in the ruined granary. The hero winds the key on Kili's back. | To find Megha, whom Kili half remembers as "my big sister in the sky". | Kili's memory comes back one piece at a time (one memory chip per world). |
| **Grandma Lakshmi** | Raman's widow and the village elder. She stays behind with the few people who did not leave. | To see the fields green again before the harvest festival. | Sends the hero a voice message at each chapter break, and is the first to plant a seed. |
| **Rao Uncle** | A jolly desert mechanic with an orange turban. | To get his old jeep, Old Ganga, back on the road. | Joins the hero in chapters 3–6 and helps rebuild the seaplane into the Kestrel jet. |
| **Selvi Akka** | A boat builder at the harbour. | To free the sea from Rust's nets. | Builds the boat *Kayal*, and later the jet with Rao. |
| **Captain Rust** | Raman's old apprentice, now a comic pirate with a crew of robots. | To prove he is a better inventor than Raman was. | Is saved by the hero in the storm. He says sorry, repairs the village well, and runs the festival fireworks. |
| **Boltbeards** | Rust's clumsy barrel robots with spring beards. | To do what Rust says, badly. | When hit they fall apart into gears and springs, which the hero collects as coins. |
| **Grumblets** | Little grumpy storm clouds that Megha sheds. | To be left alone. | When hit they burst into rain that turns the land below green. |
| **Megha** | The great rain machine in the storm. | To be repaired and to hear Kili's song again. | Her cracked face heals. She brings gentle rain home for the finale. |
| **The five Guardians** | Old creatures that each keep one Seed of Life. | To find someone patient and steady enough to carry the seed. | Each one gives its seed and returns for the finale. |
| **Biscuit** | The village dog, who waits at the gate every day. | For the hero to come home. | Is the first one to run out to greet the hero in chapter 8. |

### The five Guardians and their seeds

| Seed | Guardian | Where | What the guardian tests | What Kili remembers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seed of Earth | **Shila**, a giant stone tortoise. Her shell *is* the Giant Gate: the three stone rings are carved on her back. | Chapter 1, the Giant Road | Patience: turning the rings slowly and exactly | "Raman carved these rings. I sat on his shoulder." |
| Seed of Sun | **Sona**, a golden desert fox | Chapter 3, an old stepwell (baoli) under the desert highway | Careful counting and waiting for the right moment | "Megha's sun-panel came from this desert." |
| Seed of Water | **Neela**, a great blue whale caught in Rust's nets | Chapters 4–5, the harbour and the open sea | Steady grip and release when freeing her from the net | "Rust was Raman's helper. He was kind once." |
| Seed of Air | **Vayu**, a great white eagle who circles the storm | Chapter 6, the sky above the clouds | Remembering and repeating patterns | "Megha is my sister. She is not angry, she is hurt." |
| Seed of Forest | **Minmini**, queen of the Glowlings | Chapter 7, the deep forest | Planning, sorting and finding the way | "The song! I remember the whole song now." |

## Chapter by chapter

Each chapter keeps the gameplay of the design document. Story, set pieces and the rest
scene are added. Every chapter has the same rhythm:

1. a short comic intro;
2. 4–6 levels;
3. a rest scene by the campfire with Kili (breathing guidance, and a postcard home);
4. the guardian or boss;
5. the comic ending, and one memory chip for Kili.

### Prologue: the night of the storm (2 minutes, no movement needed)

- **Panel 1.** Malarpuram at dusk before the storm: lanterns, green fields, Biscuit asleep
  by the gate.
- **Panel 2.** A huge dark cloud with a faint face rolls over the hills. Lightning, wind,
  and roofs flying away.
- **Panel 3.** The hero holds the granary door shut with their right arm while Grandma pulls
  the children inside. The arm is hurt.
- **Panel 4.** Morning. The fields are brown, the well is dry, and most families leave on
  bullock carts. The hero wears a sling.
- **Panel 5.** In the broken granary the hero finds Kili, switched off, and winds the brass
  key. Kili's eyes open: "Hello! ...Where is Megha?"
- **Panel 6.** Grandma Lakshmi gives the hero Raman's old map. It shows five glowing seeds
  across the land. "Bring them home, and the rain will follow."
- **First movement (teaches tap):** the hero gently taps Kili's key three times to wind
  it. This is the first touch on the board, with no failure possible.

### Chapter 1: The Giant Road (on foot)

- **Story:** The only road out is the ancient Giant Road, blocked by the storm's boulders.
  At its end stands a giant stone gate that nobody has opened in a hundred years.
- **Set pieces**
  - *Boulder run:* boulders with bright cracks roll slowly down. Tapping the crack breaks
    each one into coins. Big boulders need a spark stone, which is a board placed on the
    spot.
  - *Star rain:* every 60 seconds a shower of stars. Sweep the hand across them.
  - *Six chests:* the gate key is in one of six carved chests. Kili shows the key's shape.
  - *The gate wakes up:* the gate opens one eye. It is Shila the tortoise. "Turn my rings,
    little one. Slowly." Turn the board to turn each ring until the symbols line up. The
    last ring turns the other way.
- **Rest scene:** a campfire under a banyan tree. The hero's sling comes off, and a green
  wrist band goes on.
- **Reward:** the Seed of Earth; the old bicycle in the gatehouse; memory chip 1.

### Chapter 2: Hill Trail (bicycle)

- **Story:** The bicycle is rusty. The hero must cross the tea hills before sunset,
  because after dark the trail is lost in fog.
- **Set pieces**
  - *Chain repair:* tap the chain parts in the order Kili shows (3, then 4, then 5 parts).
  - *Tea-hill ride:* turn the board to steer on winding trails, hold to pedal uphill, and
    tap to hop over logs.
  - *Race the sunset:* the sky changes from gold to pink to purple during the level. Smooth
    steering earns more lantern-coins, which light the trail as night falls.
  - *Firefly bridge:* the last bridge has missing planks. Place a board on each lit gap.
- **Rest scene:** a tea-picker family shares chai. A postcard home shows the hill.
- **Reward:** a map piece showing the desert town and a jeep; memory chip 2 is found inside
  the bicycle bell.

### Chapter 3: Desert Highway (jeep)

- **Story:** Rao Uncle's jeep, Old Ganga, will not start: Rust has bought the only fuel
  station and put coin locks on every pump.
- **Set pieces**
  - *Highway:* steer around cactus, goats and sand drifts. Sandstorms hide the road for a
    few seconds, and Kili's voice guides the lane.
  - *Coin locks:* each pump shows a sum. Answer by placing that many boards, or by tapping
    the answer. The sums grow from counting to addition, then subtraction, then money.
  - *Fill to the green:* hold to fill the tank and let go on the green mark.
  - *The stepwell:* a sandstorm reveals an old stepwell under the dunes. Sona the fox leads
    the hero down, step by step (sequence), to the Seed of Sun and a chest of gold coins.
- **Rest scene:** a night under desert stars. Rao Uncle tells how Raman fixed his jeep
  thirty years ago.
- **Reward:** the Seed of Sun; gold coins (the hero can now buy a boat); memory chip 3.

### Chapter 4: The Harbour (rowing boat)

- **Story:** The harbour is full of tangled nets with Rust's cog symbol on them. Selvi Akka
  sells the hero her best boat, *Kayal*, for the gold.
- **Set pieces**
  - *Rowing:* the left hand rows the left oar and the right hand the right oar. Taking turns
    goes straight, and one side turns the boat.
  - *Fishing:* stand a board to drop the line and lift it at the right moment to catch fish.
  - *Lighthouse code:* remember the lighthouse's colour pattern and row to the island that
    shows the same pattern.
  - *Neela in the nets:* a huge whale is trapped. Pull each net peg free with a steady grip
    (lift the board). Neela sings and swims off: "Follow me when the pirates come."
- **Rest scene:** a fish curry on the beach with Selvi. *Kayal* gets a small foam-ball
  cannon.
- **Reward:** the boat cannon; memory chip 4 ("Rust was kind once").

### Chapter 5: Pirate Attack (boat battle)

- **Story:** Captain Rust's ship, the *Clanking Crab*, attacks to take the gold. "That gold
  belongs to the greatest inventor alive. ME!"
- **Set pieces**
  - *Robots on deck:* Boltbeards climb over the rail on every side. Tap them where they
    appear, and they fall apart into gears.
  - *Foam cannon:* turn the board to aim, stand a board to load, and tap to fire.
  - *Shield or splash:* Kili warns of incoming balls. Hold the shield only for red balls and
    let blue water balloons through (go/no-go).
  - *Boss, the four-wheel lock:* the *Clanking Crab*'s hull lock has four wheels. Turn each
    one to the angle shown, and Neela rises and lifts the ship's wheels out of the water.
    Rust escapes in his seaplane, which crashes softly on a sandbank, and Rust swims away.
- **Rest scene:** Rao and Selvi rebuild the seaplane into the jet *Kestrel*.
- **Reward:** the Seed of Water (Neela gives it); the Kestrel; memory chip 5.

### Chapter 6: Storm Skies (Kestrel jet)

- **Story:** The storm cloud sits over the mountains. Inside it is a floating fortress of
  cloud and brass, and at its heart is Megha.
- **Set pieces**
  - *Through the clouds:* turn one board to bank and tap with the other hand to fire
    sun-pulses at Grumblets. Each one bursts into rain, and the brown land below turns green.
  - *Ring run:* fly through glowing rings by touching where each ring is.
  - *Saving Rust:* Rust is trapped in the storm in a little balloon basket. Steering close
    enough lets Kili drop him a rope. He says nothing and looks ashamed.
  - *Megha:* her six petal shields open in a pattern. Repeat the pattern (3 to 7 steps).
    There is no destruction. Each correct pattern repairs one crack on her glass face. At
    the end Kili sings Raman's song, and Megha opens her eyes and smiles.
  - *The last bolt:* one last wild lightning bolt hits Kestrel. The hero jumps out with the
    parachute. Vayu the eagle catches Kili and gives the hero the Seed of Air in mid-air.
- **Reward:** the Seed of Air; memory chip 6 ("Megha is my sister").

### Chapter 7: Parachute and Forest Survival

- **Story:** The hero lands in a deep forest at dusk and has lost Kili. The Glowlings find
  the hero first.
- **Set pieces**
  - *Parachute:* turn to guide the canopy, tap the stars that fall past, and land on the
    glowing clearing.
  - *Camp:*
    - Lift boards to "chop" fallen wood.
    - Place boards in a triangle to build the fire.
    - Sort berries, tapping only the ones that Kili's voice described (Kili is heard through
      the trees).
    - Build a shelter piece by piece.
  - *Night path:* Glowlings light a path through the trees. Remember it, and in the morning
    walk it again from memory to find Kili caught in a vine.
  - *Raft:* build a bamboo raft and steer it down the river, through rapids and past
    sleeping crocodiles that only yawn.
  - *Queen Minmini:* she asks three sorting riddles, then gives the Seed of Forest. From the
    raft the hero sees smoke from a village chimney. It is Malarpuram.
- **Reward:** the Seed of Forest; memory chip 7 (Kili remembers the whole song).

### Chapter 8: Home (the farm and the festival)

- **Story:** Biscuit runs out barking with joy. Grandma Lakshmi cries. The fields are still
  dry, and only a few families are left.
- **Farming loop** (from the design document):
  1. Plough: sweep across the rows.
  2. Sow: one board per seed hole, following a colour pattern.
  3. Water: turn the well wheel, then hold to pour.
  4. Protect: tap the crows away gently.
  5. Harvest: lift the boards as crops ripen.
  6. Market: count and sell.
- **Story beats:**
  - *Planting the five seeds:* each seed is planted in the village square by the person who
    helped find it. Grandma plants the Seed of Earth.
  - *Megha comes home:* the first gentle rain in a year falls on the square.
  - *Rust returns:* he arrives on foot with his robots, carrying the well's missing wheel.
    "I broke her. Let me help fix things." The village lets him stay.
  - *The village fills up:* every harvest brings one family home. Their house is repaired,
    and one more market stall and one more lantern appear.
  - *Festival:* a harvest festival in the style of Pongal, with kolam patterns, a pot boiling
    over, dancing and Rust's fireworks. Then the statue is unveiled, "Hero of Malarpuram",
    showing the player's chosen hero with Kili on their shoulder.
- **After the ending:** home practice mode. Daily farm tasks follow the therapy plan, the
  seasons change (sowing, rain, harvest, festival), and Kili brings a new postcard from one
  of the guardians each week.

## How each act maps to SITAR

The design document assumes holes, pegs and a rotation encoder. SITAR is a flat touch
table with three-legged pegboards (cup, cone and so on), so each gesture becomes:

| Story gesture | On SITAR |
|---|---|
| Tap | Touch the target on the table |
| Peg place (spark stone, plank, seed) | Stand the named board on the target spot (the game knows which board it is) |
| Peg pull (net, wood, harvest) | Lift a standing board straight up after it has been held |
| Rotate (rings, steering, aiming) | Turn a standing board on the table; the game reads its angle |
| Hold (pedal, shield, fuel) | Keep a finger or a board still on the pad |
| Sweep (stars, plough) | Slide a hand across the table along the lit path |
| Two-board (rowing, jet) | One board or hand on the left half and one on the right half |

## Tone rules for writing lines

- Kili speaks in short, warm lines of 8 words or fewer, for example "Turn it slowly. Good!",
  "Almost! Once more." or "Look, the land is turning green!"
- Nobody is ever hurt or killed. Robots pop into gears, clouds burst into rain and rocks
  break into coins.
- Failure is never named. The scene slows down, Kili points, and the target stays.
- Every chapter ends with something growing.
